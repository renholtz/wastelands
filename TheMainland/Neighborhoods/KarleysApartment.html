… The night at the bar with the other soldiers was nice. Walking Riegel home was nice. Going home was not nice. Karley enjoyed her privacy, enjoyed her space. But at home where she was granted an abundance of both was where things got complicated. She had just made herself something to eat to burn off the last of the alcohol in her system, had just made her way down the hall towards her bedroom. … The door across the hall always stayed open, though she never stepped foot in the room. She didn’t belong there, it had remained untouched as much as possible. Karley hated wasting space. She needed to move soon. She didn’t need a two bedroom apartment. Being a soldier kept her life comfortable, but downsizing made the most sense now. … But she could make it a guest room, couldn’t she? Or maybe an entertainment room, or an office? It’s just a room without a soul, with no purpose anymore. It sits full of belongings Karley feels she has no right to touch, to decide what’s worth discarding or keeping for herself. Staring into the darkness she tries to picture what this space could become, but all she sees is nothing. But at least it’s open, it needs to be. She can’t forget. How many empty rooms exist in the mainland? … She wonders about her coworkers, the closest people in her life. Do they live in haunted apartments as well? Do they stare at the darkness before them, and does it stare back? Or do they look away, bury that empty space away as far back as possible until it’s as though it never existed at all? She can’t imagine that. To be haunted means to be bound, tied to something that now only exists in memories, and in dreams, and in grief. … Work will get busy soon, people will be dependent on her actions. She decides the best thing she can do for her squadron is to rest her body. Sleep doesn’t come quick, but she submits to the quiet all the same.

The day is winding down. Karley had the day off work. What had she gotten up to? She sat cross-legged in the middle of her bed and closed her eyes, reflecting on the day. Had it been productive enough? Had she done enough to earn a proper rest yet? ... She had cleaned the apartment from top to bottom, gone grocery shopping, and rearranged the kitchen cabinets. She'd gone for a jog around the neighborhood and eaten three proper meals. Physically, she felt great; fulfilled, accomplished. Mentally? Harder to place. Not bad. Not good, either, but comfortable enough. This was her baseline, she figured. ... Yes. That would do. She could enter leisure mode now. She let herself fall backward onto the bed, stretching her arms overhead as a quiet sigh escaped her. What would be fun right now? ... ... She read for a while—maybe an hour—just enough to get her mind moving without becoming fully absorbed. Satisfied, she slipped a bookmark between the pages and set the book aside before standing to stretch out her hamstrings. The kitchen awaited. Tonight she'd allow herself a treat. This was her ‘self-care’ time, after all. She usually tried to limit how much fresh produce she bought. She made decent enough money but she often worried about developing expensive tastes. Even so, while she'd been shopping earlier, she hadn't been able to resist, the ripest peach she’d ever seen.. She sliced into it with ritualistic precision, exactly as she'd been taught as a child. The knife glided around the pit before she separated the fruit into eight perfectly even wedges, arranging them neatly on her nicest plate. While she was in the kitchen, she opened the junk drawer and retrieved one of her more treasured possessions, an old world deck of playing cards. A kettle whistled softly. She poured herself a cup of tea. This would be the perfect night. She settled into her desk chair, placing the plate of peaches beside the steaming mug. With practiced hands, she shuffled the cards until they snapped cleanly beneath her fingers. She set up a game of Solitaire. She cracked her knuckles, ready to begin. Just as she reached for the first card— Knock. Knock. Her head snapped toward the front door, the relaxed expression disappearing from her face. Why would someone be visiting this late? Something must have happened again. She hurried to answer. August stood on the other side with his usual bright smile. "Why are you here?" The question came out flat. He didn't seem bothered by the cold reception. "Karley! It's been a while. I, uh... just wanted to stop by and check on you." "...Okay?"
