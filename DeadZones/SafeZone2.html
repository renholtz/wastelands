During the first team meeting, Hekla and Riegel talked everyone through the expected project workflow. The main experimental setup will be growing sample plants in different types of fertilizer, analyzing the results, and using these results to inform new fertilizer development. You will test the new fertilizers and repeat the process. Toward the end of the week, the team put together the experiment for what will be the first time of many. It’s a simple setup; you are growing lima bean plants in different soil samples. For the purpose of gathering baseline data during this first iteration, the soil samples do not contain any experimental fertilizers and are just from three different areas: • An area of the farm with dying crops • An area of the farm with healthy crops • A Luxizor greenhouse. The greenhouse soil is acting as the control. There are multiple cups of soil (and bean seeds) set up per area, as a larger sample size will help reduce the margin of error. Obviously, you don’t yet have the data from this round of testing. But Hekla, Noa, Abel, Selene, and Tessa were still able to analyze at least the soil and some crops last week–this gave the team enough information to create two new types of fertilizer to be tested. Following Selene’s theory that perhaps the plants have just grown “needier,” Fertilizer A has a higher concentration of every macronutrient a plant needs. Fertilizer B addresses Abel’s begrudging suggestion that the plants could be suffering from an iron deficiency. Iron deficiency effects are visible in young plants; if left untreated, the deficiency progresses to necrosis. Thus, Fertilizer B contains elevated iron levels while otherwise maintaining the same nutrient levels as the existing standard fertilizer. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
